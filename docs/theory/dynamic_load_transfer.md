# Phase 3D: Quasi-Static Four-Wheel Dynamic Load Transfer

## 1. Motivation
During vehicle maneuvers involving acceleration, braking, and cornering, inertial forces act at the vehicle's center of gravity (CG). Because the CG is located at a non-zero height ($h_{\text{CG}}$) above the ground contact plane, these inertial forces generate pitch and roll overturning moments. These moments redistribute normal vertical contact forces ($F_z$) among the four wheels. Because tire tractive and cornering force capacities scale directly with normal load ($F_z$), capturing dynamic load transfer is essential for realistic four-wheel rover simulation.

## 2. Quasi-Static Assumption
This implementation employs a **quasi-static dynamic vertical-load estimation framework**.
> [!IMPORTANT]
> This Phase 3D model estimates quasi-static vertical tire loads from vehicle acceleration. It does not model suspension dynamics, roll, pitch, transient load transfer, wheel-hop, or measured suspension kinematics.

The vehicle body is modeled as a planar 3-DOF rigid body ($v_x, v_y, r$). Vertical, pitch, and roll accelerations/velocities are not integrated as dynamic state variables; instead, pitch and roll moments are assumed to be in instantaneous static equilibrium with the restoring moments generated by tire normal forces.

## 3. Coordinate Frame & Sign Conventions
Following the project's standard ISO/body-fixed coordinate frame:
- **$+x$ (Forward):** Positive longitudinal acceleration ($a_x > 0$) causes pitch moment that transfers normal load from the front axle to the rear axle.
- **$+y$ (Left):** Positive lateral acceleration ($a_y > 0$, turning left) causes roll moment that transfers normal load from the left (inside) wheels to the right (outside) wheels.
- **$+z$ (Upward):** Normal loads $F_z$ represent positive compressive upward ground reaction forces ($F_z > 0$).
- **Wheel Indexing:** $1 = \text{FL}$ (Front-Left), $2 = \text{FR}$ (Front-Right), $3 = \text{RL}$ (Rear-Left), $4 = \text{RR}$ (Rear-Right).

## 4. Static Load Distribution
Under gravitational equilibrium with total vehicle mass $m$ and gravitational acceleration $g$:
$$F_{z,\text{total}} = m \cdot g$$
$$L = l_f + l_r$$

Axle static normal loads based on longitudinal CG placement:
$$F_{z,\text{front,static}} = m \cdot g \cdot \frac{l_r}{L}$$
$$F_{z,\text{rear,static}} = m \cdot g \cdot \frac{l_f}{L}$$

With a centered lateral CG, static normal load is distributed symmetrically between left and right wheels:
$$F_{z,\text{FL,static}} = F_{z,\text{FR,static}} = \frac{F_{z,\text{front,static}}}{2} = \frac{m \cdot g \cdot l_r}{2 L}$$
$$F_{z,\text{RL,static}} = F_{z,\text{RR,static}} = \frac{F_{z,\text{rear,static}}}{2} = \frac{m \cdot g \cdot l_f}{2 L}$$

For the baseline symmetric rover ($m = 60.0\,\text{kg}, g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2, l_f = l_r = 0.40\,\text{m}$):
$$F_{z,\text{FL,static}} = F_{z,\text{FR,static}} = F_{z,\text{RL,static}} = F_{z,\text{RR,static}} = 147.15\,\text{N}$$

## 5. Longitudinal Load Transfer Derivation
Longitudinal acceleration $a_x$ generates an inertial force $m a_x$ at the CG height $h_{\text{CG}}$, producing a pitch moment about the ground contact plane:
$$M_{\text{pitch}} = m \cdot a_x \cdot h_{\text{CG}}$$

Equilibrium of pitch moments about the contact patch yields the total axle longitudinal load transfer:
$$\Delta F_{z,\text{long}} = \frac{m \cdot a_x \cdot h_{\text{CG}}}{L}$$

Assuming equal left/right distribution across each axle:
- **Front axle reduction:** $\Delta F_{z,\text{long,front}} = -\Delta F_{z,\text{long}} \implies \Delta F_{z,\text{long,FL}} = \Delta F_{z,\text{long,FR}} = -\frac{m \cdot a_x \cdot h_{\text{CG}}}{2 L}$
- **Rear axle gain:** $\Delta F_{z,\text{long,rear}} = +\Delta F_{z,\text{long}} \implies \Delta F_{z,\text{long,RL}} = \Delta F_{z,\text{long,RR}} = +\frac{m \cdot a_x \cdot h_{\text{CG}}}{2 L}$

## 6. Lateral Load Transfer Derivation
Lateral acceleration $a_y$ generates an inertial force $m a_y$ at the CG height $h_{\text{CG}}$, producing a total roll moment about the roll axis:
$$M_{\text{roll,total}} = m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}}$$

## 7. Front/Rear Lateral Transfer Allocation
In the absence of a detailed suspension roll-stiffness model, the total roll moment is allocated between the front and rear axles in proportion to their static vertical load fractions:
$$\lambda_f = \frac{l_r}{L}, \quad \lambda_r = \frac{l_f}{L}$$

The front and rear axle roll moment shares are:
$$M_{\text{roll,front}} = \lambda_f \cdot m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}} = \frac{l_r}{L} \cdot m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}}$$
$$M_{\text{roll,rear}} = \lambda_r \cdot m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}} = \frac{l_f}{L} \cdot m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}}$$

Equilibrium of roll moments across the independent front ($t_f$) and rear ($t_r$) track widths determines the lateral load shift per wheel:
$$\Delta F_{z,\text{lat,front}} = \frac{M_{\text{roll,front}}}{t_f} = \frac{l_r}{L} \cdot \frac{m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}}}{t_f}$$
$$\Delta F_{z,\text{lat,rear}} = \frac{M_{\text{roll,rear}}}{t_r} = \frac{l_f}{L} \cdot \frac{m \cdot a_y \cdot h_{\text{CG}}}{t_r}$$

## 8. Individual Four-Wheel Load Equations
Combining static, longitudinal, and lateral contributions yields the individual wheel vertical forces:

$$F_{z,\text{FL}} = \frac{m g l_r}{2 L} - \frac{m a_x h_{\text{CG}}}{2 L} - \frac{l_r}{L} \frac{m a_y h_{\text{CG}}}{t_f}$$
$$F_{z,\text{FR}} = \frac{m g l_r}{2 L} - \frac{m a_x h_{\text{CG}}}{2 L} + \frac{l_r}{L} \frac{m a_y h_{\text{CG}}}{t_f}$$
$$F_{z,\text{RL}} = \frac{m g l_f}{2 L} + \frac{m a_x h_{\text{CG}}}{2 L} - \frac{l_f}{L} \frac{m a_y h_{\text{CG}}}{t_r}$$
$$F_{z,\text{RR}} = \frac{m g l_f}{2 L} + \frac{m a_x h_{\text{CG}}}{2 L} + \frac{l_f}{L} \frac{m a_y h_{\text{CG}}}{t_r}$$

## 9. Vertical-Load Conservation Checks
The load transfer model strictly redistributes load without creating or destroying vertical force:
$$\sum_{i=1}^4 F_{z,i} = F_{z,\text{FL}} + F_{z,\text{FR}} + F_{z,\text{RL}} + F_{z,\text{RR}} = m \cdot g$$
For any valid $a_x$ and $a_y$, the sum of longitudinal transfer terms is zero ($-\Delta F_{z,\text{long}} + \Delta F_{z,\text{long}} = 0$) and the sum of lateral transfer terms is zero ($-\Delta F_{z,\text{lat,front}} + \Delta F_{z,\text{lat,front}} - \Delta F_{z,\text{lat,rear}} + \Delta F_{z,\text{lat,rear}} = 0$).

## 10. Physical Validity and Wheel Lift Rejection
A ground tire cannot support tensile normal force ($F_z \le 0$). When extreme longitudinal or lateral accelerations exceed the rollover/pitch-over threshold, the quasi-static formulation predicts $F_{z,i} \le 0$ (wheel lift).

The implementation strictly rejects non-positive normal loads:
$$\min(F_{z,i}) \le 0 \implies \text{Error: } \texttt{dynamicNormalLoadDistribution:negativeNormalLoad}$$
This prevents unphysical negative normal forces from propagating into downstream Magic Formula tire models.

## 11. Representative $h_{\text{CG}}$ Parameter Provenance
> [!WARNING]
> The center of gravity height $h_{\text{CG}} = 0.30\,\text{m}$ is a representative modeling parameter chosen for mathematical characterization and verification. It is not an experimental measurement of a physical commercial rover.

## 12. Limitations
- **Quasi-Static Approximation:** Omits suspension spring-damper dynamics, suspension compliance, suspension geometry (anti-dive, anti-squat, roll center heights), and transient body oscillations.
- **Axle Roll Allocation:** Uses static weight distribution ($\lambda_f, \lambda_r$) rather than experimentally identified front/rear roll stiffness ratios ($K_{\phi,f}, K_{\phi,r}$).
- **Planar Body Assumption:** Pitch and roll angles are assumed small ($\cos\theta \approx 1, \cos\phi \approx 1$).

## 13. Future Extensions

- Integration of dynamic normal loads into the closed-loop 3-DOF rover simulation.
